Math Teaching & Tutoring

My teaching philosophy centers on three convictions:

  1. That the right amount of productive struggle is what transforms students into creative and resilient thinkers,
  2. That mathematics becomes sticky when it carries meaning (when matrices are verbs, when epsilons are enemies to be defeated...), and
  3. That a classroom is at its best when everyone, including me, is comfortable being wrong out loud.

The most underrated skill in mathematics is knowing how to learn it. I try to make visible the strategies that mathematicians have internalized so deeply they've forgotten they're using them — seeking out the simplest possible example, running sanity checks, knowing when to hunt for a different textbook or a better search term. To a student, when we skip straight to the insight and hide the fumbling that preceded it, the whole thing looks like impossible magic. I'd rather show them the fumbling.
