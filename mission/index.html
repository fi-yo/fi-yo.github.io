Mission

We're living through a strange and underappreciated shift. For the first time in history, answers are essentially free. Explanations are instant. Information, once the scarce resource that education was built around, is now abundant to the point of noise. What's become rare—and therefore worth something—is the capacity to know what to do with it. Judgment. Discernment. The ability to ask the right question in the first place.

That shift has forced me to think more carefully about what education is actually for. I never believed it was simply about transferring knowledge—but I think most systems behave as though it is. The result is a generation of people who are well-informed and underprepared. Who can retrieve answers but struggle to build arguments, make decisions, or tolerate uncertainty. The problem was always structural, but AI has made it impossible to ignore.

What I keep coming back to is this: the experiences that actually shape people—that build confidence, agency, and a sense of direction—are almost never the ones that can be systematized. They happen in conversation. In the friction of a hard problem. In the particular relationship between someone who knows something and someone who's trying to become something. That dynamic is old, and it still works, and I think it's what's been quietly missing from modern education.

So I'm building toward that. A startup that treats learning as something closer to apprenticeship—small groups, real projects, mentors who are genuinely invested. Work that produces something: research, writing, tools, products that exist beyond the classroom and mean something outside of it. AI is woven into how we operate, not as a crutch but as infrastructure—something that handles the mechanical and frees up space for the harder, more human work.

The goal isn't scale for its own sake. It's depth. I want to build something where progress is legible not because it's been measured, but because it's visible—in how someone thinks, how they communicate, what they choose to work on next. Where the question isn't "what did you score?" but "what did you make, and what did it cost you to make it?"

I don't think most people know what they're capable of. I want to build something that helps them find out.
