Mission

We're living through a strange and underappreciated shift. For the first time in history, answers (and even explanations!) are essentially free and instant. Information, once the scarce resource that education was built around, is now abundant to the point of noise. What's become rare—and therefore worth something—is the capacity to know what to do with it. The ability to ask the right question in the first place. Let's call it… taste.

I never believed education was simply about transferring knowledge, but most systems behave as though it is. They produce people who can name every planet in the solar system but have never been asked whether Pluto deserves to be on the list. Who can recite the Pythagorean theorem but have no idea they're using it every time they cut across the crosswalk. Who can identify a logical fallacy by name but have never been asked to change someone's mind. And that's just the ones who passed. The problem was always structural, and now AI has made it impossible to ignore.

What I keep coming back to is this: the experiences that actually shape people are almost never the ones that can be systematized. They happen in conversation, in the friction of a hard problem, in the particular relationship between someone who is something and someone who's trying to become something. That dynamic is old, and it still works, and its absence is the gaping hole at the center of modern education.

So that's what I'm building. I named it Scholē—the ancient Greek word for leisure, and the root of the word "school." In ancient Athens, scholē was what you did once the hard physical labors of life were taken care of: the time left over for thinking, debating, becoming. That idea feels newly relevant now that AI is absorbing so much of the mechanical labor of knowledge work. Scholē is a marketplace that connects learners directly with mentors, cutting out the institutions that have long decided what's worth pursuing and who's qualified to pursue it.

I don't think most people know what they're capable of. I want to build something that helps them find out.
